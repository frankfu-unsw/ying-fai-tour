How this was built
  • Flats 9 and 10 sit at the tip of Block B's east wing. Their living-room windows face east, like flats 2 and 3 in Block A. The Block B floor plan is drawn with north pointing right, so the windows sit at the bottom of that drawing.
  • The Block B plan shows no acoustic fins beside flats 9 and 10, so only the window frame limits the view.
  • Floor heights assume Block B matches Block A floor for floor: 2/F at +57.55 mPD and 2.75 m per floor, eye level 1.5 m above the floor. Block B's own cross-section was not available. If its floors sit a little lower, read each result about one floor higher.
  • The terrain is public SRTM-based elevation data at about 30 m resolution near the site and 100 m further out. Sea beyond 7 km east is assumed open. Dark blue patches in the overview mark the sea the chosen window can see.
  • Block positions come from the brochure's key plan, accurate to roughly 5 to 10 m. The side and bay windows of each flat are not modelled.
  • Nearby buildings, including the village houses on the Silverstrand slope and the film studio, are drawn as simple blocks from OpenStreetMap footprints and floor counts (heights are guessed where unknown). They are there for context only: the sea figures ignore them, as they do trees unless you tick the tree option, so on the lower floors check the window view for houses in the way. Earth curvature is included.
  • Main roads come from OpenStreetMap and are laid on the terrain surface, so flyovers and cuttings show at ground level. Tunnels are left out.
