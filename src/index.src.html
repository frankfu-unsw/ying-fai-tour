How this was built
  • Walls, rooms and windows follow the HA typical floor plans: Block A 3/F to 39/F for flats 2 and 3, Block B 4/F to 38/F for flats 9 and 10. Flat 3 is drawn as a mirror image of flat 2, and flat 10 of flat 9, as the plans show. Sizes come from the dimension strings; window sill and head heights are estimates.
  • Floor levels: 2/F is at +57.55 mPD and each floor adds 2.75 m; Block B is assumed to match Block A floor for floor. Eye level is 1.55 m above the floor. 2/F in Block A and 3/F in Block B are drawn with the typical layout.
  • Plans A, B and C add light 75 mm partitions. In plan B the master bedroom's new wall meets the east window part-way along its north half, so it has to land on a window frame post; where the real posts are may move it by a few hundred millimetres. Any partition needs HA approval under the flat's decoration guidelines before you build it.
  • A room with no window, like the helper's room in plan B, needs mechanical ventilation such as an extractor fan or air-con.
  • The acoustic fin beside flat 2 sits in line with its north wall and sticks out 1.5 m past the east facade on every floor; flat 3's is on its south side and stops at 25/F. Flats 9 and 10 have none.
  • Outside, the terrain is SRTM-based elevation data at about 30 m resolution near the site and 100 m further out. Nearby buildings are simple blocks from OpenStreetMap footprints and floor counts, for context only; they, trees (unless ticked) and islands more than 7 km away do not count in the sea figures.
  • Both towers are traced from the HA typical floor plans and placed from the brochure's key plan; the lifts and refuse rooms come from the same plans. The podium deck (1/F, taken at about +52 mPD), the lobbies, the roof features and the planters are estimates from the layout plan and the HA's model. Main roads come from OpenStreetMap and are laid on the terrain, so flyovers and cuttings show at ground level; tunnels are left out. Zoom out, or pick Area, to see them with the nearby places.
  • "Degrees of sea" counts the compass directions in which you can see sea through a window from where you stand, after walls, the fin, the neighbouring flats and the hills. Window figures are taken about 1.5 m back from the glass where the room allows. Bedroom and bathroom doors count as shut: inside one of those rooms only its own window counts, and from elsewhere you can't see through it.
