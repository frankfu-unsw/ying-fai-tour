How this was built
  • Walls, rooms and windows follow the HA typical floor plans: Block A 3/F to 39/F for flats 2 and 3, Block B 4/F to 38/F for flats 9 and 10. Flat 3 is drawn as a mirror image of flat 2, and flat 10 of flat 9, as the plans show. Sizes come from the dimension strings; window sill and head heights are estimates.
  • Floor levels: 2/F is at +57.55 mPD and each floor adds 2.75 m; Block B is assumed to match Block A floor for floor. Eye level is 1.55 m above the floor. 2/F in Block A and 3/F in Block B are drawn with the typical layout.
  • Plans A, B and C add light 75 mm partitions. In flats 9 and 10 the new walls meet the east window at its middle pier; in flats 2 and 3 they meet it at the middle post, and in plan A the wall between the two small bedrooms needs a new frame post where it meets the north window (south in flat 3). Where the real posts are may move a wall by a few hundred millimetres. Any partition needs HA approval under the flat's decoration guidelines before you build it.
  • The acoustic fin beside flat 2 sits in line with its north wall and sticks out 1.5 m past the east facade on every floor; flat 3's is on its south side and stops at 25/F. Flats 9 and 10 have none.
  • Outside, the terrain is SRTM-based elevation data at about 30 m resolution near the site and 100 m further out. Nearby buildings are simple blocks from OpenStreetMap footprints and floor counts, for context only; they, trees (unless ticked) and islands more than 7 km away do not count in the sea figures.
  • Both towers are traced from the HA typical floor plans and placed from the brochure's key plan; the lifts and refuse rooms come from the same plans. The podium deck (1/F, taken at about +52 mPD), the lobbies, the roof features and the planters are estimates from the layout plan and the HA's model. Main roads come from OpenStreetMap and are laid on the terrain, so flyovers and cuttings show at ground level; tunnels are left out. Zoom out, or pick Area, to see them with the nearby places.
  • "Degrees of sea" counts the compass directions in which you can see sea through a window from where you stand, after walls, the fin, the neighbouring flats and the hills. Window figures are taken about 1.5 m back from the glass where the room allows. Bedroom and bathroom doors count as shut: inside one of those rooms only its own window counts, and from elsewhere you can't see through it.
  • The sun's position is worked out for the estate (about 22.31°N, 114.27°E) on Hong Kong time with the standard solar formulas, which are good to a fraction of a degree. The direct-sun times treat the flat's own walls, the acoustic fin and both towers as solid from the ground to the roof, and take the hills from the same terrain as the views; nearby buildings, trees (unless you add them), cloud and haze are left out. The light and shadows in the 3D view come from the same sun, and in the dollhouse the flat's ceiling still blocks the light even though you can't see it.
  • The rest of Hong Kong in the area view (all of it, including Lantau, the airport, Tuen Mun, Yuen Long and the outlying islands) is loaded by your browser when the page opens: the terrain from the AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen, mostly SRTM, the same source as the nearby terrain), and the coastline, reservoirs, main roads and built-up areas from OpenStreetMap through OpenFreeMap's OpenMapTiles vector tiles. Place names and peak heights are also from OpenStreetMap. Built-up areas are estimated from how dense the roads are, so treat them as a rough guide. Zoomed in, the buildings round the point you're looking at stream in from the same OpenStreetMap data, with heights where they are mapped, and the names of estates, buildings and roads come from the Lands Department's map labels (© Map information from Lands Department). If they can't be loaded, the area view shows only the Tseung Kwan O area.
